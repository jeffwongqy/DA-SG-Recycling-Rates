## Introduction 
Recycling plays an important role in Singapore's efforts to reduce waste and promote environmental sustainability. Different materials, such as paper, plastic, metal and glass, may have varying recycling rates due to differences in usage, collection and recycling processes. This project aims to examine how recycling rates for these four materials have changed in Singapore over time. Using a synthetic dataset and data visualisation techniques, the analysis will compare recycling rates across materials, identify trends and explore changes in recycling performance over the years. The findings aim to provide a clearer understanding of recycling patterns and highlight the importance of sustainable waste management in Singapore.

## Conclusion

Overall, recycling success depends heavily on the type of material used. Metal is the most reliable material, maintaining a high recycling rate of around 91% and leaving minimal waste in landfills. While paper makes up the largest volume of recycled waste at 4.9 million tonnes, its recycling rate has dropped steadily over the past decade. Meanwhile, plastic poses the biggest environmental challenge, showing the lowest recycling rate at around 8%, a downward trend, and the highest disposed waste at 9.5 million tonnes. To improve sustainability and reduce landfill waste, businesses should shift away from single-use plastics, prioritize easily recyclable materials like metal, and focus on reducing overall packaging waste at the source.
