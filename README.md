## Introduction 
Recycling plays an important role in Singapore's efforts to reduce waste and promote environmental sustainability. Different materials, such as paper, plastic, metal and glass, may have varying recycling rates due to differences in usage, collection and recycling processes. This project aims to examine how recycling rates for these four materials have changed in Singapore over time. Using a synthetic dataset and data visualisation techniques, the analysis will compare recycling rates across materials, identify trends and explore changes in recycling performance over the years. The findings aim to provide a clearer understanding of recycling patterns and highlight the importance of sustainable waste management in Singapore.

## Analysis Questions

### 2. Which material contributes the most to total recycled waste?

### 3. How have recycling rates changed from 2015 to 2025?

<img width="1248" height="495" alt="BQ3" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/63284bb5-7baf-4662-b47f-4043145cfa02" />

From 2015 to 2025, metal recycling held steady at around 90–91% while paper dropped by nearly 19 percentage points from 50% to 31%—and glass and plastic stayed consistently low below 20% and 10%—showing that businesses should shift packaging away from declining paper and low-performing plastic toward metal to achieve reliable, long-term sustainability targets.

### 4. How are recycling rates distributed across the four materials?

### 5. Which material has the highest amount of waste disposed?

Plastic accounts for the highest disposed waste at around 9.5 million tonnes—over 35% higher than paper's 7.0 million tonnes and more than 40 times metal's 0.2 million tonnes—highlighting a major landfill burden that businesses can reduce by cutting single-use plastics and switching to reusable or highly recyclable metal alternatives.

## Conclusion

Overall, recycling performance depends heavily on material type. Metal is the most reliable material, holding a steady 90–91% recycling rate from 2015 to 2025 while leaving minimal landfill waste. Although paper accounts for the largest volume of recycled waste at 4.9 million tonnes, its annual recycling rate dropped sharply from 50% to 31% over the decade. Glass also remained low under 20%, while plastic posed the biggest challenge—staying below 10% in recycling and generating the highest disposed waste at 9.5 million tonnes. To meet long-term sustainability goals and cut landfill waste, businesses should reduce single-use plastics, prioritize reliable materials like metal, and focus on reducing overall packaging at the source.
